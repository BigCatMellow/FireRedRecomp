# Balance Philosophy

## Primary design rule

> **Community ideas expand the hypothesis space. The class/move data decides what is actually broken.**

Community hacks, Smogon discussions, and player complaints are useful counterfactuals. They are not authority.

## Intervention order

Before changing base stats, check in this order:

1. move quality;
2. move access and learnset timing;
3. type move ecosystem;
4. resource friction/scarcity;
5. physical/special category behavior;
6. evolution and acquisition timing;
7. utility/setup/team contribution;
8. only then targeted base-stat changes.

This prevents a move-distribution problem from being "solved" with permanent stat inflation.

## Role model

Primary role classes used by the research:

1. Physical Attacker
2. Special Attacker
3. Mixed Attacker
4. Fast Attacker
5. Physical Wall
6. Special Wall
7. General Tank
8. Status / Utility
9. Setup Attacker
10. Glass Cannon
11. Bulky Attacker
12. Specialist / Gimmick

A species may have a secondary role. Role labels are descriptive constraints, not power tiers.

## Progression bands

- EARLY: roughly levels 5–15
- EARLY_MID: 15–25
- MID: 25–35
- LATE: 35–45
- END: 45–55+

Use the form actually available in that band. Do not evaluate a level-35 team using final-form stats for a Pokémon that cannot exist yet.

## Type identity: affordances, not hard assignments

Preferred hierarchy:

> **Type → role affordances**  
> **Species → actual role**  
> **Moves/stats/ability → execution**

Types should have tendencies, but a type should not force every member into one job.

Examples:

- Psychic — special offense, control, setup, screens.
- Bug — setup, disruption, physical pressure, status, unusual mechanics.
- Poison — attrition, status, disruption, mixed bulk.
- Rock — physical bulk, slow offense, disruption.
- Ground — physical offense, durability, control.
- Fighting — physical offense, setup, anti-defense.
- Electric — speed, special offense, paralysis/control.
- Grass — status, recovery, sustain, special offense.
- Fire — pressure, burn, mixed/special offense.
- Water — broad generalism, bulk, special offense, utility.
- Flying — speed, physical pressure, tempo.
- Normal — generalism, utility, broad but costly coverage.
- Ghost — disruption, immunities, status, unusual offense.
- Ice — high offensive pressure, fragile/specialist.

## Parallel ladders inside a type

Do not judge a type only by "best damaging STAB."

A healthy type ecosystem can have several parallel ladders:

- damage;
- control;
- defense;
- setup;
- sustain;
- team enabling;
- specialist/conditional tools.

A species should consume only the branches appropriate to its identity.

## Intentional asymmetry vs accidental deficiency

This distinction is central.

**Intentional asymmetry:** Bug has fewer straightforward nukes because its ecosystem emphasizes powders, trapping, multi-hit pressure, setup, disruption, and unusual mechanics.

**Accidental deficiency:** Bug attackers are weak because usable Bug attacks simply do not exist at meaningful points in progression.

The mod should preserve intentional asymmetry and repair accidental deficiency.

## What "balanced" means here

Balanced does **not** mean equal.

A lower-BST Pokémon can be healthy if it has:

- useful timing;
- a coherent niche;
- meaningful utility;
- setup access;
- speed/control leverage;
- team synergy;
- resource efficiency;
- a distinct matchup profile.

The question is:

> **Does this Pokémon have a coherent reason to exist on a team without erasing another Pokémon's identity?**
